# Style guide

How a human-made PwC proposal deck reads and flows. Fill in the brand blanks marked TODO
with your own template details. Keep this file short; detail belongs in the pattern library.

## Storyline
- Default proposal flow (change it to match `storylines.md` when the client's RFP asks for a set order):
  1. Title
  2. What we heard: the client's situation in their words and numbers
  3. Objectives: what success looks like, measurable
  4. Our approach: phases, activities, timeline
  5. Deliverables and governance
  6. Team
  7. Why us: specific credentials, not adjectives
  8. Commercials and next steps (only if asked)
- If the RFP lists evaluation criteria or a required structure, follow it exactly, using the RFP's own section names.
- A section divider before each major section. 10-20 content slides for a typical proposal.
- Build the storyline from headlines first. Every content slide's headline makes one point, and the headlines read in order tell the story.

## Rhythm and variety
- Never use the same pattern on two slides in a row (section dividers aside).
- When a section has two slides of the same kind (two objectives slides), use different patterns for them.
- Rotate the objectives patterns across decks. Do not default to the 3-column layout every time.
- Mix dense and light slides. After a busy slide, the next one should breathe.

## Density
- Respect the word capacity in `catalogue.md`. When content does not fit, split the slide or cut, never shrink fonts.
- 3-5 items per slide. More than 5 means two slides or a table.
- Body boxes: 1-3 short sentences or 2-4 bullets.

## Tracker, sources, notes
- Every content slide carries the section name in the tracker slot.
- Every figure has a source line on its slide.
- Put the talk track (what the presenter says) in speaker notes, not on the slide.
